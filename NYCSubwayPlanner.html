About the Planner

An unofficial helper for getting around New York City by subway: every route in the five boroughs, including the shuttles and the Staten Island Railway. Enter your location by GPS, address or station, choose a destination, and get a step-by-step guide to which trains to take, which platform, express or local, where to change, and what it costs.

How route finding works

Location → Best Station
The Haversine formula gives the distance from your GPS position or address to every station. The planner tries the nearest few (up to 6 within walking range) and picks whichever gets you there soonest once walking time is counted, so a slightly further station on the right line beats an extra change.
The service running now
Which trains run changes through the day: the 2 and A make local stops late at night, the 5 ends at Bowling Green at weekends, the B and W run on weekdays only, the M runs to Forest Hills only on weekdays. The planner uses every stop pattern in MTA's timetable for the current time band (weekday rush hours, middays, evenings, Saturday, Sunday or late night), so it never sends you on a train that isn't running and tells you where to change instead.
Dijkstra's Shortest Path
Ride times are MTA's timetabled stop-to-stop times and waits are half the time between trains, so a route with frequent trains beats one with long gaps. Changing has a real cost: across the platform at an express stop, or MTA's minimum transfer time between lines in a complex like Times Sq or Fulton St. Dijkstra's algorithm then finds the quickest journey, with a small extra penalty per change to prefer direct routes.
Which train
Each step names the platform as the station signs do (“Uptown & The Bronx”, “Manhattan”, “Coney Island”), every route whose trains make the same stops (“2 or 3”), whether that's the express or the local, and trains to let pass, such as an A to Lefferts Blvd when you need Far Rockaway.

Fares

One fare covers any subway trip, however far, with free changes between trains. Pay by tapping a contactless card, phone or OMNY card (MetroCards can no longer be bought or refilled). After 12 paid rides in any 7 days the rest of that week's rides are free, as long as you use the same card or device. You also get one free transfer to or from a local bus within 2 hours. Two out-of-system transfers are free as well: Lexington Av/59 St ⇄ Lexington Av/63 St and Junius St ⇄ Livonia Av. On the Staten Island Railway fares are only collected at St George and Tompkinsville, and the Staten Island Ferry is free. Reduced fares are for seniors and people with disabilities.

Good to know

Hours
The subway runs 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Late at night (about midnight to 6 AM) trains come every 20 minutes or so, many routes make local stops, and some (B, C, W, Z and the diamond expresses) don't run.
Planned work
The planner follows MTA's regular timetable. Planned work often changes service, especially at weekends and late at night, so check mta.info or the posters in the station before you travel.
What's covered
Every subway route, including the 42 St, Franklin Av and Rockaway Park shuttles and the rush-hour diamond 6 and 7 expresses, plus the Staten Island Railway, linked to Manhattan by the Staten Island Ferry. Not covered: buses, PATH, LIRR, Metro-North, NYC Ferry and the JFK AirTrain (change at Howard Beach or Sutphin Blvd–Archer Av).
Data and accuracy
Everything comes from MTA New York City Transit's open data: the subway GTFS feed (stop patterns, timetables, transfers and track shapes) and the MTA Subway Stations table (station complexes and the platform direction signs). Journey times are timetabled times plus average waits, so they're estimates.
